# What If GTA6 Cheat Codes Worked in Real Life?

Slug: gta-cheat-codes. Part of the five-video "GTA in Real Life" series (24 hours, physics, hospital bill, 5-star wanted level, cheat codes).
Cast: you (narrator, second person), Mo (best friend), Ellie (little sister), Mum, the man in the gray suit, the bank teller.
Original cast and invented, generic cheats only: no real game codes, logos, characters, map or music.

## c01 Cold open

You fall down all fourteen stairs. You land on your head. And you stand up without a scratch.
You open your sock drawer, and there's a quarter of a million dollars in it. Again.
You press five buttons, and a bright red sports car drops out of the sky onto your driveway.
And this morning, you flew to school. Over the traffic. With a jetpack.
Five days ago, you bought an old game controller at a yard sale for two dollars. Taped to the back was a list. Twelve cheat codes. And one more line at the bottom, scratched out so hard the pen went through the paper.
So what if video game cheat codes worked in real life? Not in a game. In your street. On your body. In your bank.
And what if every single one of them came with a catch?

## c02 The rules

It starts on a Saturday. The controller doesn't plug into anything. It doesn't even have batteries.
But when you press the first code on the list, just to see, something chimes. And right in front of your face, in glowing letters only you can see: Cheat activated. Invincible.
You poke your arm with a fork. Nothing. You drop a dictionary on your foot. Nothing. Not even a red mark. You throw yourself down all fourteen stairs, on purpose. It doesn't even tickle.
The rules are on the back of the list, in tiny, old handwriting.
One. A cheat stays on until you enter the same code again.
Two. Nobody else can see the letters.
And three. Never, ever enter the last code.
Under all the scribbles, there's one word. You can't quite read it. Not yet.

## c03 The fun part

Monday. Mo bets you can't skate down Dead Man's Hill. Nobody has ever made it to the bottom.
You don't make it either. You hit the curb at full speed, fly over a parked van and land face first in a hedge.
You stand up. You pick a leaf out of your hair. Mo drops his phone.
"Do it again."
Tuesday, you try code number four. Money. A soft chime, and a sports bag drops onto your bed. Inside, twenty-five stacks of hundred-dollar bills. Two hundred and fifty thousand dollars.
You enter it again. Another bag. By dinner, you've stopped counting bags and started counting rooms.
You buy pizza for the whole school. Eleven hundred pizzas. The delivery driver cries a little.
It has been raining for three days, so you try code number two. Sunshine. The clouds just slide apart over your town, and only your town. On TV, the weather lady stops mid-sentence and stares at her map.
Wednesday. Code number seven. A low rumble, a flash, and a red sports car drops out of thin air onto your driveway. Brand new. Keys in. You're fourteen. You can't drive. You make another one anyway. In blue.
Ellie wants a pony. There's no pony code. She settles for a car.
Thursday. Code number nine. Jetpack. It arrives with a bang, strapped to your back, and you go straight up so fast your shoes stay on the lawn.
You fly to school above the traffic jam, over the bus, where everyone presses their faces to the windows, over the principal's car, and land on the school roof just as the bell rings.
Mo is waiting. He's been waiting since seven.
"Okay. Okay. Jetpack. Me next."
For four days, you're the luckiest person who has ever lived.
Then, on Friday morning, you smell something burning. And you look down.

## c04 The catch

It's your sleeve. You leaned on the stove while you made toast, and your sleeve has been on fire for about a minute. You didn't feel a thing.
That's the problem with invincible. Pain isn't your body being mean. It's an alarm.
And there are real people who are born without it. Doctors call it congenital insensitivity to pain. Kids who have it bite their tongues, walk on broken bones and burn themselves without knowing, because nothing tells them to stop.
Your body can't be hurt. But everything you wear, hold or lean on still can.
Then there's the money. You take one stack to the bank to open an account. The teller holds a bill up to the light. Then another. Then she lays them side by side. Her smile doesn't move. But her other hand slides under the desk.
Every dollar bill has its own serial number. Yours all have the same one. Every bag the cheat gives you is the same bag, copied. So legally, you didn't get rich. You made counterfeit money. A quarter of a million dollars of it. Forty times.
The cars are worse. They have to come from somewhere. Einstein showed that mass and energy are the same thing. To make one car out of nothing, about fifteen hundred kilos, you'd need around a hundred and thirty-five billion billion joules. That's about a fifth of all the energy the whole world uses in a year.
And every time you press that code, every light on your street flickers.
And the jetpack. Real jetpacks exist. The best ones fly for about ten minutes before the fuel runs out. Yours never runs out.
Which is how, on Thursday afternoon, you end up three thousand feet up, in your socks, next to a plane.

## c05 Someone notices

The pilot reports it as soon as he lands. A person in a jetpack, next to a passenger jet.
That has actually happened. In twenty twenty, airline pilots flying into Los Angeles reported a person in a jetpack at about three thousand feet. The FBI went looking.
That night, the jetpack is on the news. Mo sends you the clip with forty-two crying-laughing faces.
Across town, an engineer at the power company is staring at a graph. Three huge power dips this week. All of them from one street. Yours.
And there's one more phone call. The bank teller makes it.
In America, fake money isn't just a job for the police. It's a job for the Secret Service. The same people who guard the President. They were started in eighteen sixty-five for one reason: to catch people making fake money.
Friday evening, three black cars park outside your house, right behind the three sports cars that shouldn't exist.
A man in a gray suit rings the doorbell. He holds up two bills. Same serial number.
"These are very good," he says. "Too good. Nobody can print these. So how are you doing it?"
Behind him, a van with a satellite dish parks across the road.
Your phone buzzes. It's Mo. "Dude. Why is there a helicopter over your house?"
And the man in the suit looks right past you, at the controller on the kitchen table.

## c06 The choice

"We'll take that," he says. "And you'll come with us. Just for a few questions."
A few questions. You know what that means. You're the only person on Earth who can make money, cars and energy out of nothing. Nobody is letting you go home.
Mum steps between you and the man. "He's fourteen," she says. "He's not going anywhere." Two more men get out of the cars.
Ellie is on the stairs in her pajamas. She's crying.
You can't run. And invincible doesn't help when the problem isn't hurting you. It's taking you. You could make a hundred bags of money, or a hundred cars. Nothing on that list makes people forget.
So you grab the controller, run up to your room and lock the door. You pull out the list and press it flat against the window.
And with the evening sun shining through the paper, you can finally read the word under the scribbles.
Reset.
Downstairs, the front door opens. Footsteps on the stairs.
"Don't do anything stupid," the man says through the door.
Ellie says your name. Your phone buzzes again. Mo. "Whatever you're about to do, don't."
You don't know what reset means. Reset the cheats? Reset the house? Reset you?
But it's the only code left that might fix everything.
So you enter it. Red. Red. Down. Blue. Up.

## c07 Game paused

There's no chime this time. Just silence.
Ellie's voice stops halfway through your name. The man's hand stops on the door handle. Outside, a bird hangs in the air, wings wide open, not moving.
The whole world is paused. Everything except you.
In front of your face, in the same glowing letters: Game paused.
Then, across the street, a front door opens. Someone walks out into the frozen evening, holding a controller exactly like yours.
They look up at your window. And they wave.
Who else has a controller? How long have they had it? And what happens when someone presses play?
Is any of this real? The cheats aren't. But every catch is. People born without pain really do get hurt without noticing. Every dollar bill really does have its own serial number. One car made from nothing would cost a fifth of the world's energy for a year. And pilots really did see a jetpack next to their plane.
